1950
Global CO2 emissions: about 6 billion tons a year. The postwar industrial boom is just getting started.
Pollution is still mostly local — factory smoke, coal soot — not yet a planetary problem. Wildlife populations are still largely intact.
1970
The first Earth Day. 20 million people turn out. Emissions: 15 billion tons a year, and rising.
Smog over major cities is now thick enough to see from space. Species are already vanishing at roughly 100x the natural background rate.
1990
The IPCC's first report confirms what scientists already knew. Emissions: 22 billion tons a year.
Acid rain and a hole in the ozone layer are both daily headlines. The Amazon is losing over 1% of its forest every year.
2000
Global CO2 emissions: 25 billion tons a year.
Global plastic production has passed 200 million tons a year. Wildlife populations are already down roughly 30% since 1970.
2010
Global CO2 emissions: 33 billion tons a year. Arctic sea ice is now declining at over 12% a decade.
Ocean dead zones — mostly from fertilizer runoff — have roughly quadrupled since 1950.
2019
Global CO2 emissions: 36.7 billion tons. The highest ever recorded, at that point. It would not stay the record for long.
Global plastic production has passed 460 million tons a year. Roughly 1 million species are now at risk of extinction.
2020
Then everyone stayed home.
Global CO2 emissions fell by roughly 5.4% — the largest annual drop ever recorded.
Nitrogen dioxide levels over major cities dropped by 30–60% within weeks, visible from satellites.
The Himalayas became visible from parts of Punjab for the first time in 30 years.
Venice's canals ran clear enough to see the bottom. Wild boar wandered Barcelona. Deer walked through English towns.
It took the entire world stopping.
2021
Emissions rebounded to 36.4 billion tons — nearly erasing the drop in under twelve months.
Wildlife populations are now down an estimated 69% since 1970 — and still falling.
2023
Global CO2 emissions: 37.4 billion tons. A new record. 2024 broke it again.
Microplastics have now been found in human blood and lungs. Coral reefs are bleaching on a global scale for the fourth time on record.
Now
Atmospheric CO2 sits above 420 parts per million — a level the planet hasn't seen in roughly 3 million years.
Species are going extinct at an estimated 1,000x the natural rate. Wildlife populations are down roughly 73% since 1970.

The planet showed us exactly what it needed. For a few months, it got it.
Then things went back to normal.
